The set of upper triangular matrices is a subgroup of GL2(R)

August 6, 2026

Proposition

Let

\[ G = \left\{ \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&c\end{pmatrix} : a,b,c\in\mathbb R,\ ac\ne0 \right\}. \]

Then G is a subgroup of GL₂(ℝ).

Proof

Since det([[a, b], [0, c]]) = ac ≠ 0 for every element of G, each such matrix is invertible. We verify the three subgroup axioms.

(1) Closure under multiplication

Let A = [[a, b], [0, c]] and B = [[d, e], [0, f]] be elements of G. Then

\[ AB = \begin{pmatrix}ad&ae+bf\\0&cf\end{pmatrix}. \]

Since a, c, d, f are nonzero, ad and cf are nonzero. Hence AB ∈ G.

(2) Identity element

The identity matrix I = [[1, 0], [0, 1]] satisfies 1 · 1 ≠ 0, so I ∈ G.

(3) Closure under inverses

For A = [[a, b], [0, c]] ∈ G,

\[ A^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac1a&-\frac{b}{ac}\\0&\frac1c\end{pmatrix}. \]

Since 1/a and 1/c are nonzero, A⁻¹ ∈ G.

Therefore G is a subgroup of GL₂(ℝ). □
